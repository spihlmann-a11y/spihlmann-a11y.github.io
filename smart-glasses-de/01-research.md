# Market Research Report: Smart Glasses (Consumer & Enterprise, AI/AR) in Germany

*Research date: 20 November 2026. Sources are mostly from 2025-2026. Figures marked "estimate" are inferences, not published data. Unit numbers from analysts are shipments to retailers (sell-in), not consumer sales.*

---

## Executive Summary

- **The category is growing fast and Meta dominates it.** Worldwide smart-glasses shipments grew 139% YoY in H2 2025 (Counterpoint). Western Europe accounted for **30%** of global shipments, which makes it the No. 2 region after North America. Meta (with EssilorLuxottica: Ray-Ban/Oakley) holds roughly **69-82%** of the market, depending on the source and period.
- **German consumers are split.** In Bitkom's August 2026 survey, **43%** would wear smart glasses (15% already have), while **52%** rule it out. **76%** do not want to be filmed by them, and **82%** want a clearly visible recording indicator.
- **Privacy is the biggest risk and the biggest opening in Germany.** In 2026 there was a public fight over whether camera glasses count as illegal "disguised recording devices" under **§ 8 TDDDG**:
  - The Bundesnetzagentur (BNetzA) currently does **not** plan a ban, because it considers the LED indicator sufficient.
  - The Hamburg data protection commissioner calls a ban "quite possible".
  - HateAid filed a criminal complaint against Meta and German retailers (Fielmann, Apollo, Mister Spex, MediaMarkt). The ZIT cybercrime unit opened a preliminary review.
  - The EDPB issued a statement on smart glasses on 13 Oct 2026 that puts GDPR duties on **manufacturers**, not only on wearers.
- **Opening for a new entrant.** A "privacy-by-design" product could stand out: an obvious recording indicator, on-device processing, no bystander data used for AI training, and possibly a display-first or camera-less design. So could an enterprise or B2B focus, where co-determination and GDPR processes are already established.

### 1.2 Key trends
1. **AI glasses without a display now drive volume.** Camera + microphone + speaker glasses with voice AI (Meta AI, Gemini, ChatGPT) are where the volume is. Meta AI has supported **German** since 2025 (heise), which removed a major adoption barrier in Germany.
2. **Display and AR glasses are the next wave.**
   - Meta Ray-Ban Display: German pre-orders from 23 Sep 2026, retail sales from 13 Oct 2026. First physical stores are in Frankfurt, Hamburg and Munich (retail-news.de).
   - Rokid launched in Germany on 29 Apr 2026: AI Glasses Neo at EUR 449 and Rokid Glasses with display at EUR 699 (heise).
   - Even Realities G2 is sold in Germany from about EUR 699 (heise Preisvergleich).
3. **Big tech is entering.**
   - Samsung/Google Android XR glasses (Warby Parker / Gentle Monster frames) launch first in the US and Korea. No EU date has been confirmed (Wareable, Android Authority).
   - Apple's display-less glasses are reportedly targeted for 2027. This is a rumour (Counterpoint/9to5Mac).
4. **Optical retail is becoming the main channel.** Fielmann has sold Ray-Ban Meta in about 200 of its roughly 1,300 stores since August 2026. Apollo, Mister Spex and MediaMarkt/Saturn also sell smart glasses (drweb.de, heise). Meta is pushing prescription "Optics" lines (IDC).
5. **Growth is slowing in 2026.** IDC cut its forecast, and Q2 2026 growth was "only" 35%. The market is moving from a hype phase into a phase of fighting for distribution and differentiation.
6. **Policy headwinds in Germany and the EU (see Section 4):**
   - § 8 TDDDG debate
   - EDPB statement on smart glasses (Oct 2026)
   - Big Brother Award 2026 (technology category) given to Fielmann/Apollo for selling camera glasses
   - Petitions from NGOs

### 1.3 Subsidies / funding
- **No subsidy specifically for smart or AR glasses was found** for consumers or companies in Germany.
- **Mittelstand-Digital (federal):** The current network of Mittelstand-Digital Zentren is being phased out by the end of 2026. A new funding call for a successor network was published in Dec 2025 (BMWE). The centres offer free advice, demos and pilot projects for SMEs, which can be a useful entry point for enterprise smart-glasses pilots.
- **State programmes** can fund SME digitalisation investments, which could include AR glasses for service or logistics. Examples: Bavaria "Digitalbonus" (runs to 31 Dec 2027), Saarland "DigitalInvest" (up to EUR 20,000), Hesse "Distr@l", Brandenburg "Innovationsgutschein", Saxony-Anhalt "Digital Innovation" (lexware.de overview). Eligibility is decided case by case.
- **R&D funding:** Generic R&D programmes such as ZIM (Zentrales Innovationsprogramm Mittelstand) and EU Horizon Europe may apply to product development. These were not specifically checked for 2026 calls.

## 3. Target Customer Profile

### 3.1 Consumers (B2C)
| Segment | Profile | Needs / pain points | Buying criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Tech early adopters** | Mostly male, 16-40, urban | Hands-free capture, AI assistant, music/calls | Features, design, price EUR 300-700 |
| **Sport / outdoor** (cycling, running, skiing) | Active, high income | POV video, audio, navigation, no phone in hand | Robustness, battery life, sport integrations |
| **Prescription-glasses wearers** (about 2/3 of adults in Germany wear glasses; estimate based on industry figures, not verified for 2026) | All ages, buy through opticians | "If I wear glasses anyway, they may as well be smart." Need prescription lenses and in-store fitting | Comfort, weight, available lenses, optician advice |
| **Accessibility users** (low vision / blind, hard of hearing) | Health- and need-driven | Object and text recognition, live subtitles (e.g. Be My Eyes integration, live transcription) | Reliability, German-language quality, possible reimbursement (health-insurance reimbursement not investigated) |
| **Travellers / multilingual users** | Business and leisure travel | Live translation | Translation quality, data security |

**Attitudes in Germany (Bitkom, Aug 2026, n=1,164, aged 16+):**
- 15% have already used smart glasses. 28% would like to. 52% rule it out.
- 76% reject being filmed unnoticed. 62% would not let people wearing smart glasses into their home.
- 82% want a clearly visible recording indicator.
- Earlier Bitkom data: 26% (2025) and 37% (Aug 2026) can imagine wearing AR glasses. The 2025 headline was "4 in 10 are interested in smart glasses".

**What this means:** Interest is substantial, but **social acceptance** from bystanders is the main limit on adoption. Privacy-friendly design is part of how the product earns the right to be bought in Germany.

### 3.2 Businesses (B2B / enterprise)
| Segment | Use cases | Decision-makers | Buying criteria |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Manufacturing / mechanical engineering (Mittelstand)** | Remote expert support, maintenance, step-by-step instructions, quality inspection | Head of service, plant manager, IT, **works council (Betriebsrat)**, data protection officer | ROI (fewer technician trips), robustness, integration (SAP, MES), GDPR, security |
| **Logistics / warehousing** | Vision picking, hands-free scanning | COO, logistics manager, works council | Picking speed and error rate. Vendors claim workflows are up to 37% faster (TeamViewer, a vendor claim). Shift-long battery life. Ergonomics (DGUV) |
| **Energy / utilities / field service** | Remote inspection (wind farms, grids) | Head of service | Explosion protection (ATEX), connectivity, documentation |
| **Healthcare** | Documentation, telemedicine, training | Hospital IT, medical staff | Data protection for health data (Art. 9 GDPR), hygiene, possible medical device (MDR) classification |
| **Public sector / police / fire services** | Situational awareness, documentation | Public procurement | Data sovereignty (EU hosting), procurement law, BSI requirements |

**Enterprise pain points:**
- **Co-determination:** Under § 87(1) no. 6 BetrVG, the works council has a say on any technical device that can monitor employees, so a works agreement is effectively mandatory.
- **Ergonomics and occupational safety:** DGUV studies look at cognitive load and posture.
- **Proof of ROI** and the risk of getting stuck in pilots.
- **Cloud data transfers to the US/China**, which require Schrems-II / Data Privacy Framework (DPF) assessments.

Bitkom reported that about one in five companies uses VR/AR applications (2024 figure, cited secondary).

## 4. Barriers to Market Entry and Opportunities

### 4.1 Regulatory barriers

**A. Germany-specific: camera glasses as "disguised recording devices" (the biggest risk)**
- **§ 8 TDDDG** (Telecommunications Digital Services Data Protection Act) prohibits making, owning and selling transmitting devices that are disguised as everyday objects and can secretly record images or sound. **§ 27 TDDDG** makes violations punishable by up to 2 years in prison or a fine (heise, drweb.de).
- **BNetzA (Aug 2026):**
  - It sees no grounds for a ban, because the LED makes recording "sufficiently recognisable".
  - It is running no formal proceeding but is monitoring the market. It would act if there were initial grounds for suspicion.
  - It has **not** physically tested the Gen 2 model or assessed the AI functions.
  - Meta has reportedly admitted the LED stays off when certain AI features are used.
  - Sources: heise 15 Aug 2026, wespeakiot, boerse-express.
- **Hamburg data protection commissioner (Thomas Fuchs):**
  - Says covert filming in public clearly violates data protection law.
  - Calls a ban "quite possible".
  - According to Tagesschau as cited by t3n, his office is already acting against Meta and imposing fines. heise reports only public statements, so this is unconfirmed.
- **HateAid criminal complaint (Aug 2026):**
  - Filed against Meta, Ray-Ban and Oakley executives and against retailers Fielmann, Apollo, Mister Spex and MediaMarkt.
  - The ZIT (Generalstaatsanwaltschaft Frankfurt) has opened a **preliminary review**.
  - The BNetzA's view does not bind the criminal case.
- **Other relevant laws:**
  - § 201 StGB (confidentiality of the spoken word, which makes audio recording critical)
  - § 201a StGB (taking pictures that violate the most personal sphere of life)
  - § 22 KUG (right to one's own image)
- **What this means for a new entrant:**
  - A camera product needs a **clearly visible indicator that cannot be disabled** (LED plus sound), which must also be active in AI modes.
  - The product should look visibly different from ordinary glasses.
  - It should block the camera if the indicator is covered.
  - Retailers may become cautious about listing camera glasses because of the complaint against them.

**B. EU data protection (GDPR / DSGVO)**
- **EDPB Statement 01/2026 on smart glasses (13 Oct 2026, preliminary and non-binding)**, summarised by ppc.land and A&O Shearman:
  - Manufacturers and AI service providers can be **controllers** for bystander data. They cannot shift transparency duties onto wearers through their terms of service.
  - Recommended measures:
    - LEDs that work in many lighting conditions, with no recording when the LED is covered
    - Audio signals
    - Data minimisation
    - On-device processing
    - Blurring bystanders' faces
    - No persistent storage by default
    - Geofencing in sensitive places
  - Using bystander images for **AI training** under "legitimate interest" would be "particularly difficult" to justify.
  - **Facial and voice recognition** creates a "high risk" in public spaces.
- In practice, a **data protection impact assessment (DPIA, Art. 35 GDPR)** is needed. Cloud AI processing outside the EU needs a legal basis for third-country transfers.

**C. EU AI Act**
- **Banned since 2 Feb 2025:**
  - Untargeted scraping of facial images to build facial recognition databases
  - Emotion recognition in the **workplace** and in education (critical for enterprise glasses)
  - Real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces for law enforcement (with narrow exceptions)
- **General-purpose AI (GPAI) obligations since 2 Aug 2025.** These matter if your own models are used. When third-party LLMs are used, the obligations fall mainly on their providers.
- **Transparency duties (Art. 50) and high-risk rules from Aug 2026 onward.** Biometric identification or categorisation, and AI in HR or worker management, can be high-risk. The EU "Digital Omnibus" proposal (Nov 2025) proposed postponing parts of the high-risk deadlines. **Check the current status.**
- **What this means:** Do not build facial recognition of third parties into the product. Do not offer emotion recognition for enterprise customers.

**D. Product compliance / CE marking (standard EU requirements, as known; verify current versions of the standards)**
- **Radio Equipment Directive (RED) 2014/53/EU:**
  - Bluetooth/Wi-Fi radio, EMC, safety and SAR (body-worn)
  - Since **1 Aug 2025**, the **RED Delegated Regulation (EU) 2022/30** cybersecurity requirements apply (harmonised standards EN 18031-1/-2/-3, covering privacy and data protection for wearables).
- **Cyber Resilience Act (CRA):**
  - Reporting duties for actively exploited vulnerabilities from **11 Sep 2026** (24-hour early warning)
  - Full obligations from **11 Dec 2027**
  - Sources: Commission, Jones Day, Heuking
- **Other product and consumer rules:**
  - General Product Safety Regulation (GPSR, EU 2023/988), in force since Dec 2024
  - RoHS, REACH
  - EU Battery Regulation 2023/1542 (labelling, due diligence, removability rules from 2027; check exemptions for small devices)
  - Common charger / USB-C directive
  - **EU Data Act** (applies since 12 Sep 2025): users must be able to access data generated by connected devices
  - **European Accessibility Act** (since 28 Jun 2025): may apply to related services and apps
- **Germany-specific registrations:**
  - **ElektroG:** registration with **stiftung ear** before placing on the market, plus a take-back obligation
  - **VerpackG:** registration in LUCID
  - **BattG** / battery rules
- **Prescription lenses:** corrective glasses are **medical devices (MDR, class I)**. Working with opticians or lens partners is the practical route.
- **Enterprise:** DGUV recommendations for data glasses (workplace risk assessment), ATEX for hazardous areas, and a works agreement under BetrVG.

### 4.2 Market and channel barriers
- **Meta's dominance and distribution:** EssilorLuxottica (Ray-Ban, Oakley) plus German optical chains. New entrants need optician partners (e.g. Fielmann, Apollo, Mister Spex, independent opticians) or electronics retail (MediaMarkt/Saturn, Galaxus, Amazon.de).
- **Localisation:** German-language voice AI quality, German-language support and manuals, and the right to withdraw / warranty under German consumer law.
- **Trust and brand:** German consumers are very privacy-aware (76-82% in the Bitkom figures). Chinese brands face extra skepticism. NGOs actively campaign against camera glasses: Digitalcourage (Big Brother Award) and HateAid.
- **Price:** Mainstream prices are set at around EUR 350-550 (Meta). Display glasses sell for around EUR 450-700 (Rokid, Even). There is little room for higher hardware margins (IDC).
- **Enterprise:** Long sales cycles (works council, data protection, IT security). A mature software ecosystem is required (TeamViewer Frontline, SAP integration).

### 4.3 Opportunities
1. **"Privacy-first" positioning as the German USP:**
   - Clearly visible recording indicator that cannot be disabled
   - On-device AI
   - EU hosting
   - No training on bystander data
   - Possibly camera-optional or camera-free models (the Even Realities model)
   This matches what 82% of Germans want and what the EDPB recommends. It also reduces § 8 TDDDG risk.
2. **Enterprise and assisted reality in the German Mittelstand:** remote service, maintenance and logistics, where data protection and co-determination processes are already established. Partnerships with TeamViewer or SAP partners, and using Mittelstand-Digital and state SME digitalisation funds for pilots.
3. **Optical retail as a channel:** Optician chains want new product categories. A product with a less sensitive privacy profile could be attractive to them, especially now that retailers are named in the HateAid complaint.
4. **Display and translation use cases:** Display AI glasses grew +449% in H1 2026. Live subtitles and translation for people who are hard of hearing, travellers and business users are a fast-growing segment that is less tied to the camera.
5. **Accessibility:** Assistive functions for blind and visually impaired people. Health-insurance reimbursement is a possible route, but was not investigated.
6. **Timing:** Samsung/Google are not yet in the EU, and Apple is not expected before 2027. In display and AR glasses, there is still a window before the big platforms dominate.

### 4.4 Risk summary
| Risk | Likelihood | Impact |
|---|---|---|
| Ban or restriction on camera glasses in Germany (§ 8 TDDDG / criminal case) | Medium (BNetzA currently against, but criminal review and DPA pressure continue) | Very high for camera products |
| GDPR enforcement against cloud AI / bystander processing (EDPB line) | Medium-high | High |
| Meta price pressure / distribution lock-in | High | High |
| Market cool-down (growth slowing, IDC) | Medium | Medium |
| Delays in enterprise adoption (works council, ROI) | Medium | Medium |
